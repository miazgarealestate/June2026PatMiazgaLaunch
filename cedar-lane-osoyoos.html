The Distinction That Changes Everything: Freehold vs. Leasehold

In BC, manufactured home properties come in two fundamentally different ownership structures, and the difference has major consequences for financing, long-term value, and security of tenure. Understanding which type you are buying is not a technicality — it is the most important question to answer before you proceed.

Cedar Lane appears to be a freehold (or bare land strata) community, meaning buyers hold registered title to their individual lot. The home sits on land you own. When you sell, you sell both the home and the land together. A conventional mortgage lender will generally treat this the same way as any other residential real estate transaction, and the property builds equity like any other titled property.

✓ Freehold / Bare Land Strata (Cedar Lane)

  • You hold registered title to your lot at the Land Title Office
  • The home and land are sold together as real property
  • Conventional mortgage financing is typically available
  • Property appreciates in line with the broader real estate market
  • No landlord can change pad rent or terminate your occupancy
  • Estate planning is straightforward — property passes like any other real asset

✗ Leasehold Pad Rental (common alternative)

  • You own the home but rent the land from a park owner
  • No title to the land — only a tenancy agreement
  • Conventional mortgage financing is difficult or unavailable
  • Pad rents can increase, subject to MHP Act limits
  • Park operator retains control over rules and eventual land use
  • Resale market is narrower due to financing constraints

The practical effect of freehold land ownership extends to every stage of the transaction. You can finance the purchase through a conventional mortgage with standard lender terms, the same as any other residential property. The home registers as real property — not chattel — which means better rates, longer amortizations, and access to the full range of lenders rather than a specialized subset. When it comes time to sell, you are selling to any qualified buyer, not navigating the additional complexity that leasehold properties introduce.

Before proceeding with any manufactured home purchase, always confirm the title type in writing. Your REALTOR and your notary or lawyer can pull the title certificate directly from the Land Title Office. "It's in a park" does not mean it's a pad rental, and "it's freehold" is only confirmed by the title itself. At Cedar Lane, the unit number format (21-4911) is consistent with a bare land strata designation, but verification is mandatory — not assumed.

Manufactured Home Ownership in BC: What It Means

Manufactured homes in BC are built to the Canadian Standards Association (CSA) A277 standard, which governs structural, electrical, plumbing, and thermal requirements. Homes built to CSA A277 are treated comparably to site-built homes for most regulatory and financing purposes. Older homes built before the current standard was in place may have been constructed under different codes, and lenders sometimes distinguish between pre- and post-standard construction when evaluating financing eligibility.

The age and condition of the home itself carries significant weight in manufactured home transactions. A well-maintained home from the 1990s or 2000s may present no special financing challenges, while a home that is heavily dated or showing deferred maintenance could affect both the appraised value and lender appetite. Always commission a professional home inspection by an inspector experienced with manufactured construction — the inspection criteria differ from those applied to a site-built home.

BC's Home Owner Grant applies to manufactured homes on freehold or bare land strata lots in the same way as conventional housing, provided the property is your principal residence. Property Transfer Tax also applies on the same basis as other residential transactions. One tax distinction worth noting: if the home is registered as a manufactured home under the Manufactured Home Act (and not yet converted to real property), the property transfer may be structured differently. Your notary will address this at conveyance.

What to Review Before Making an Offer

A manufactured home on freehold or bare land strata land requires its own specific due diligence checklist. The items below are the ones that matter most — some are common to all residential purchases, others are specific to manufactured construction.

Title Confirmation

Pull the title search from the Land Title Office and confirm it is fee simple or bare land strata — not leasehold, not a Manufactured Home Registry registration only. If the title shows a Manufactured Home Registry number without a corresponding land title, the land situation requires further investigation. Your notary handles this as part of standard conveyancing, but ask for the title search before removing subjects, not after.

Home Age and Condition

Know the year the home was manufactured and whether it was built to CSA A277. Hire a home inspector who has specific experience with manufactured construction — inspecting a manufactured home is not identical to inspecting a site-built home, and a general inspector unfamiliar with the differences may miss issues specific to this building type. Pay particular attention to the roof, skirting, underbelly, and any site-set modifications made after the original installation.

Bare Land Strata Rules and Fees

If Cedar Lane is organized as a bare land strata, a strata corporation governs shared roads, utilities, or common areas within the development. There will be bylaws and strata fees. Review the bylaws for any restrictions on exterior modifications, parking, or suites. Confirm the monthly strata fee and review the most recent financial statements and meeting minutes, just as you would for any strata purchase.

Financing Pre-Approval

Talk to a mortgage broker before you make an offer, not after. Explain that the property is a manufactured home and provide the home's year of manufacture. Your broker will identify which lenders will consider the application and under what terms. Having this confirmed in advance means your financing subject is real protection, not a placeholder — and it tells you your actual purchase ceiling before you negotiate.
