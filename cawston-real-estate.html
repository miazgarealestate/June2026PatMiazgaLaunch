Community Spotlight — Cawston, BC — Similkameen Valley

Certified organic.
Genuinely rural.

Cawston is Canada's Organic Farming Capital — a working agricultural community five minutes from Keremeos, where roughly 40% of farms are certified organic and land still does what it's supposed to do: grow things.

Cawston sits just southeast of Keremeos on Highway 3 in the Similkameen Valley, home to a concentration of certified organic farms, orchards, and vineyards unmatched anywhere else in BC. It's an unincorporated community of under 1,000 people, built around agriculture rather than tourism — fruit-packing facilities, working orchards, and family farms that have been here for generations, plus a newer wave of organic wineries and cideries adding to the mix.

Cawston is on the traditional territory of the Lower Similkameen Indian Band, whose office is based in the community.

Why Cawston

For buyers who want working land, not a hobby

Cawston attracts a specific kind of buyer: someone who wants agricultural land that's actually productive, not just a rural address. The valley's dry climate, long growing season, and fertile soil have made it one of BC's most concentrated organic farming regions — certified organic orchards, vegetable operations, and vineyards sit side by side, many family-run for multiple generations.

It's a smaller, quieter alternative to Keremeos itself, five minutes down Highway 3, with two fruit-packing facilities anchoring the local economy and a genuine hobby-farm and horse-ranch culture. The organic wine scene here is real and growing — several wineries, including at least one with an underground tasting cave, have built reputations well beyond the valley.

This isn't resort country. There's no lake, no marina, no golf course. What Cawston offers instead is land that works, a community organized around agriculture rather than tourism, and prices that reflect a market most Lower Mainland and Alberta buyers haven't discovered yet.

🎯 Honest assessment — who Cawston is right for

If you want lake access, a walkable downtown, or resort-style amenities, Cawston isn't it — Osoyoos or Oliver will fit better.

Cawston is the right call for buyers who want: certified-organic or convertible farmland, a genuine hobby farm or horse property, proximity to Keremeos' services without living in town, or land at Similkameen pricing rather than South Okanagan pricing.

Buyers considering an active organic operation should budget time to understand certification requirements, not just soil and water — Pat can help you find the right professionals for that assessment.

What to Know

Buying in Cawston — the real considerations

🌾 Agricultural Land Reserve

Most parcels around Cawston sit in the ALR. What can be built, subdivided, or converted to non-agricultural use is restricted — always assess this before purchasing, especially if you have plans beyond the existing use.

💧 Water Rights & Irrigation

Working farms typically carry water licences and irrigation infrastructure with real value and real liability. Licence status and system condition need proper due diligence, not just a walkthrough.

🌱 Organic Certification

If continuing an existing organic operation matters to you, understand what's required to maintain certification — transition periods, record-keeping, and input restrictions all carry real cost and time.

🏥 Services

Cawston itself is small — for most services, Keremeos (5 minutes away) and Penticton (about an hour) cover the gap. Plan healthcare and amenity access around Keremeos, not Cawston proper.

🏠 Housing Stock

Farmhouses and older rural homes are common. Pat's construction background is useful here — knowing what a renovation actually costs before you commit changes the math on a purchase.

🍷 Similkameen Wine Country

Cawston sits inside BC's Similkameen Valley wine appellation, with several organic and boutique wineries nearby — a real amenity for buyers who want vineyard living without Okanagan prices.
