Osoyoos vs Calgary

Closer in price than you'd think — this move is about climate and lifestyle, not a huge equity windfall. Here's the honest comparison.

The short answer

Calgary and Osoyoos are closer in price than most Albertans expect — this isn't the dramatic equity story that a Vancouver-to-Osoyoos move is. What Osoyoos offers Calgary buyers is mainly climate: trading prairie winters and a landlocked city for a lake, a desert climate, and BC wine country, without giving up an enormous amount of equity to do it.

Pat's honest take: Calgary and Osoyoos are within about 10% of each other on overall price — closer than most people assume. This move is rarely about a huge equity gain the way a Vancouver move is. It's about climate, lifestyle, and what you want your day-to-day to look like.

Climate — the real reason Albertans move

Calgary winters are long, cold, and can swing dramatically with chinooks. Osoyoos sits in Canada's warmest, driest pocket — average summer temperature around 28°C, average winter around 1.3°C, roughly 2,039 hours of sunshine a year, and under 318mm of annual rainfall. There's no comparable body of warm water anywhere near Calgary; Osoyoos Lake is regularly cited as the warmest lake in Canada.

What's genuinely different about buying in BC

Alberta has no provincial sales tax and no land transfer tax comparable to BC's Property Transfer Tax (PTT). BC buyers pay PTT on every purchase unless a first-time buyer or newly-built exemption applies (full exemption to $835,000, partial to $860,000 for first-time buyers; full to $1.1M for newly built homes). Alberta also doesn't have anything equivalent to BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax — though it doesn't matter for an Osoyoos purchase either way, since Osoyoos sits outside the SVT's designated area. Wildfire insurance considerations and BC's mandatory disclosure rules also work differently than what Alberta buyers are used to.

Common Questions

Is Osoyoos more expensive than Calgary?

They're close. As of June 2026, Calgary's CREB® benchmark price was $572,500 versus a trailing 12-month median residential sale price in Osoyoos of about $520,000 — roughly a 10% difference, not the dramatic gap seen with Vancouver.

Why do Albertans move to Osoyoos if it's not much cheaper?

Climate and lifestyle, primarily. Osoyoos offers hot, dry summers, mild winters, and a genuine lake culture that doesn't exist near Calgary. Most Alberta buyers Pat works with are trading a comparable price point for a dramatically different day-to-day life, not chasing a discount.

Does BC have a land transfer tax like Alberta doesn't?

Yes. BC charges a Property Transfer Tax (PTT) on most purchases, generally 1% on the first $200,000 and 2% on the remainder up to $2M, with higher tiers above that. First-time buyers can get a full exemption up to $835,000 (partial to $860,000), and newly built homes have their own exemption thresholds. Alberta has no equivalent tax.

Is the Speculation and Vacancy Tax a concern for Alberta buyers in Osoyoos?

No. Osoyoos sits outside the SVT's designated taxable area, so it doesn't apply regardless of where the buyer is moving from.
